Three products

One database. Three places to work in it.

Start with the part of the day that hurts most. The other two are already reading the same records when you turn them on, so nothing has to be migrated a second time.

Mobile Office Manager™

The back office. Contracts, the MCP book, projects, purchase orders, invoicing and the dispatch board live in one browser-based ERP, so the state of a job never depends on which spreadsheet someone opened.

  • Nine connected modules: sales, contracts, projects, finance, inventory, equipment, scheduling, dispatch and analytics
  • Contracts and the MCP book held against the unit, not a customer note
  • A dispatch board you can drag, with double-booking flagged on the row
  • Purchase orders, parts and stock tied to the work order they came from
  • Analytics that drill into the records behind the number
Explore Mobile Office Manager →

Mobile Service™

What the technician carries into the machine room. The unit's history is already attached when they arrive, and the job is closed out before they leave, whether or not there is a signal down there.

  • Unit history and the last callback, open at the job
  • Today's route in the order dispatch built it
  • Parts, notes, photos and the customer's signature captured on site
  • Time recorded at the unit instead of on a sheet in the van
  • Genuine offline work in the hoistway and the machine room
Explore Mobile Service →

Customer Portal

The customer-facing side of the same database. It reads the record your office already keeps, so the questions that used to arrive as phone calls get answered at two in the morning without anyone picking up.

  • Customer Manager: equipment, locations and open work
  • Schedule Manager: upcoming visits and inspections
  • Billing Manager: invoices and payment history
  • Document Library: certificates, reports and contract paperwork
  • A separate customer sign-in, with internal workflow kept inside

Inside dispatch

GPS Tracker & Route Builder is not a fourth product.

It is the route intelligence inside scheduling and dispatch. There is nothing extra to open, no separate licence to explain to your dispatcher, and no second map to keep current.

Dispatch on where the trucks are

The board shows real technician locations, so a callback goes to the van that can actually get there. The dispatcher stops guessing which crew is closest to the building.

Routes built around the real day

Order the stops around drive time, contract windows and the category tests that are already due, then send the route straight into Mobile Service for the morning.

One record, not a second map

Locations, units and tickets come from the same database as everything else. Moving a stop on the route is a dispatch change, and the office sees it as one.

We Solve, You Succeed!

The useful part is what happens between the products.

Each of the three is worth having on its own. What makes it a platform is that a job only gets entered once, and every screen after that is reading the same entry.

The office sees what the technician captured at the unit. The customer sees it in the portal. Nobody retypes it in between.

01The job is entered once, on a serial-numbered unit. Every step after this reads that entry instead of repeating it.
02It reaches the phone as part of the day’s route, not as an email somebody has to notice and act on.
03The unit’s history arrives with it, so nobody rings the office from the machine room to ask what happened last time.
04Hours, parts, photos and the signature are recorded at the car, and no signal is required for any of it.
05Payroll, stock and billing read that same entry. Nothing is exported, imported or reconciled between them.
06The property manager sees the closed visit in the portal, because the portal is that record filtered down to what they may see.
Who opens it

Which of the three each role lives in.

Four roles touch all three products. Each one has a home screen, and none of them has to go looking in another system for the rest of the story.

For Owners

The whole business, on one screen.

Owners stay in Mobile Office Manager. Analytics reads the contracts, work orders and hours as the office and the field record them, so the dashboard is current without anybody preparing it.

  • Analytics built on live work orders
  • Contract margin, not just contract revenue
  • Branches and crews compared in one view
For Operations

Dispatch without the whiteboard.

Dispatch works the board in Mobile Office Manager while Route Builder orders the driving. Mobile Service reports each job back as it changes state, so the board is not kept current by phone calls.

  • Dispatch board with GPS routing inside it
  • MCP visits, category tests and callbacks on one calendar
  • Job status arriving from the technician’s phone
For Finance

Close the month, not the office.

Finance stops waiting for the van. Hours, parts and the signature reach the finance module the moment Mobile Service syncs, which turns billing into a review rather than a transcription.

  • Time sheets that arrive already costed
  • Recurring maintenance billing from the contract
  • Invoices raised against the closed work order
For Property Managers

The portal your customers ask for.

Property managers never sign in to the ERP. They get the Customer Portal, which is the same records again with everything internal left behind the office login.

  • Their own equipment list, always current
  • The next inspection date without a phone call
  • Invoices and certificates they can download

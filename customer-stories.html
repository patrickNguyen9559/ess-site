How it happened

Friday stopped being a paper day.

Nothing about the work changed. The technicians still ride the same routes, open the same machine rooms and answer the same callbacks. What changed is where the hours are written down, and how many times a person has to touch them before payroll can run.

01Before: time sheets were filled in by hand in the van, sometimes days after the visit, and handed to the office at the end of the week.
02The office spent a full day reconciling them. Which building was that block of hours against? Which work order? Was that overtime or drive time?
03Now the technician logs time in Mobile Service at the unit, against the customer, the location and the work order it belongs to, while standing in front of the job.
04It syncs into Mobile Office Manager as data, not as paper to be keyed in. The machine room has no signal, so the app records offline and sends when the tech is back above ground.
05The same trip carries the rest of the visit home: parts used, notes, photos and the customer’s signature. Billing can go out while the visit is still fresh.
06The technicians kept using it, which is the part Linda did not take for granted. Unit history and the last callback are on the phone at the car, so nobody rings the office for a building contact any more.

The pattern

What the four stories have in common.

An app the technicians abandon by the third month saves the office nothing. These crews still open it in the machine room, and that is where the office hours come back from.

Industry · Overhead door

Commercial door contractors service openings, not addresses.

Sectional doors, rolling steel, dock equipment and gate operators sit across a customer's sites, and every one of them has its own history. ESS keeps the record at the opening, so the office and the truck are looking at the same door.

The work

A door that has failed shut stops the building behind it.

Planned maintenance rounds and emergency callbacks, often at the same customer in the same week. Serial-numbered equipment, a documented service cadence and contracts that run for years. That is the pattern ESS was built for.

01

Sectional and rolling doors

Springs, cables, tracks, panels and operators. Each door carries its own cycle count, its own repair history and its own parts list, and none of that belongs in a technician's memory.

02

Dock levelers and restraints

Levelers, vehicle restraints, seals and shelters. Dock equipment is the part of the building the customer notices the moment it stops working, and the part that gets serviced under contract.

03

High-speed doors

Cold storage, clean rooms and heavy traffic openings. A controller fault turns into a production problem inside an hour, so the technician needs the last fault and the last parts fitted before he arrives.

04

Gate operators

Slide and swing operators, loops, safety edges and access controls on the perimeter. Usually a different schedule from the doors inside the building, usually on the same contract.

05

Planned maintenance rounds

One customer, several buildings, a list of openings. The round is built from the contract, so the technician works down the list and the office knows what was covered on each visit.

06

Emergency callbacks

A door has failed shut and a truck is waiting to load behind it. Dispatch the nearest technician, hand him the door's history on the way, and keep the callback attached to the contract it belongs to.

What ESS gives the door contractor

The record lives at the opening. The invoice goes out the same day.

Four things change in the first month on the platform.

Equipment records by opening

Every door, leveler, restraint and operator is its own record, filed under the building and the bay it serves. Serial number, model, install date, parts fitted, every visit since. A technician who has never been to that site opens the door's history before he gets out of the truck.

Photos and signatures captured on site

Mobile Service captures the damaged panel, the bent track and the customer's signature at the opening. The photos land on the work order instead of a phone gallery, and the office has the proof before the technician reaches the next stop.

Parts from the truck, reconciled against stock

Truck stock is inventory. When a spring, a cable or a seal comes off the van it is charged to the job and taken off the truck count, so replenishment is a report rather than an argument at the end of the month.

Invoice the same day

Labor, parts and travel reach the office as the technician closes the ticket. Service calls are billed that afternoon instead of the following week, and contract work stays on its own billing schedule.

Call to invoice

One pass through the office. Nothing typed twice.

The same job, from the phone call to the paid invoice, without a paper ticket crossing anyone's desk.

01The call comes in. The caller is a contract account, so the sites, the openings and the terms are already on file.
02Dispatch sends the nearest truck. The GPS Tracker and Route Builder inside dispatch assigns the technician who can actually get there first, not the next name on the board.
03The technician opens the door's history on the phone. Previous repairs, parts fitted and the last maintenance visit are there, and they stay there when the signal drops inside the building.
04Work is captured at the opening. Time, parts, photos and the customer's signature go on the ticket before the technician leaves the site.
05The office bills it. Nothing is re-keyed, and the property manager sees the visit, the documents and the invoice in the Customer Portal.

Industry · Fire & life safety

Inspection-driven work, code-bound records, contracts measured in years.

A fire and life safety contractor does not sell a visit. It sells a cycle that repeats for the length of a multi-year agreement, against devices that have to be found, tested and written up the same way every time.

Book a demo Explore the platform
The rhythm

Four cycles running at once, and every one of them comes off the contract.

Annual, semi-annual, quarterly, monthly. Each frequency starts as a line on a signed agreement, and each one has to reach a route before it is late.

01The contract sets the cadence. A building signs for a term, and the term carries the frequencies: annual on one device class, semi-annual on another, quarterly here, monthly there. None of that should live in one coordinator's head or in a spreadsheet tab only she knows how to sort.
02The visits repeat all year. The same site comes back month after month, often with a different scope each time. Dispatch has to know which devices are in scope for this visit, who is qualified to do it, and how it stacks with the other buildings on that route.
03The inspection finds something. Deficiencies get written against the device that has them, and the real ones should become quoted repair work while the customer still remembers the conversation, not stay a line on a form nobody prices.
04The paperwork has to leave the building. A visit produces a report, and usually a tag or certificate. It has to reach the building owner, and it has to be findable when the authority having jurisdiction asks. A copy in the glovebox of a service truck is not a record.
05Then the year starts again. Renewal comes round while last year's reports are still the ones being requested, so the contract, the device list, the history and the documents all have to still agree with each other.
What ESS does about it

The schedule and the record, held in one place.

ESS does not decide what the code asks of you. It keeps the cycle on a board, the equipment in a list and the documents where the customer can reach them.

01

Equipment records at device level

An extinguisher, a panel, a pump, a sprinkler riser or an alarm device is a record of its own, with location, make, model and serial where you carry it. History attaches to the device rather than to an invoice, so "when was this one last tested, and by whom" is a lookup instead of a hunt through old paperwork.

  • Make, model, serial and location per device
  • Inspection and repair history on the device itself
  • Devices grouped by building, floor and riser
02

Recurring visits generated from the contract

Frequencies live on the agreement, and Mobile Office Manager turns them into scheduled visits across the year. Dispatch places them on a route with the rest of the day's work, and the technician opens the right device list for that site in Mobile Service when he arrives.

  • Annual, semi-annual, quarterly and monthly cadences
  • Visits on the dispatch board, not on a wall calendar
  • Scope and device list ready when the technician opens the job
03

Due soon and overdue, before they bite

The visit nobody has scheduled yet is the one that costs you. ESS surfaces what falls due in the coming window and what has already slipped, by building and by contract, so a coordinator can move it while it is still early instead of finding out from the property manager.

  • Due-soon views by building, contract and frequency
  • Overdue flagged, with an owner against it
  • Renewal dates visible before the term runs out
04

Documents filed against the device

Reports, tags, photos and signed sheets are stored against the equipment and the visit that produced them, then published to the Customer Portal document library. The property manager pulls last quarter's report without calling your office, and your team can produce the same file years later.

  • Reports and photos attached to the visit and the device
  • Document library in the Customer Portal for the building owner
  • One copy of the record, not four versions in four inboxes
One path

A deficiency is the start of the next job, not the end of the visit.

The inspection that finds a problem is the cheapest lead your business gets that week. It only becomes revenue if somebody prices it quickly and someone else remembers to follow it up. In ESS the finding is captured against the device, quoted from the office, scheduled as repair work and billed on the contract it came from.

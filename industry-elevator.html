The work

Every kind of work an elevator company sells, on one book of units.

You are not running a queue of service tickets. You are running a maintenance book, a test calendar that does not move, callbacks that arrive without warning, and project work that has to be costed while it is still running.

01

MCP scheduling

The maintenance control program is built once per unit and then it runs. Tasks come from the contract and the equipment record, land on the calendar, and go out on a route instead of a wall planner.

  • Task lists by unit type and controller
  • Recurring visits generated from the contract
  • Missed visits visible before the customer calls
02

Category 1 and category 5 tests

Test dates belong to the unit, not to a spreadsheet on somebody’s desktop. ESS carries the due date, the scheduling, the witness and the paperwork on the equipment record so nothing quietly goes out of date.

  • Due dates held against each unit
  • Test visits scheduled like any other work
  • Results and documents filed with the unit history
03

Callbacks

A call comes in and the ticket opens against the right building, the right car and the history that matters. Dispatch sends whoever is closest, and cause, action and parts are recorded at the unit rather than remembered later.

  • Ticket opened on the unit, with its history attached
  • Nearest available technician dispatched
  • Repeat callbacks visible by unit and by building
04

Modernization projects

A modernization is a project, not a long work order. Scope, purchase orders, material, labor and billing milestones stay attached to the job, so the cost picture is current while the crew is still in the hoistway.

  • Phased scope with billing by milestone
  • Purchase orders and material tied to the job
  • Cost to date against the estimate
05

New installation

Long lead times, staged deliveries and site readiness that is never quite what the schedule said. Installation work is tracked against the contract and then handed over cleanly into the maintenance book when the unit is turned over.

  • Submittals, deliveries and site readiness in one place
  • Labor and material charged to the contract
  • Handover into the MCP book without rekeying
06

Contracts, billing and renewals

Multi-year service agreements with escalations, add-ons and units coming on and off contract. The agreement drives the schedule and the billing run, and you can see which contracts are actually making money.

  • Escalations and renewal dates tracked by agreement
  • Invoice the same day the work is done
  • Profitability by contract, branch and crew
A day on the board

One board for route work, category tests and callbacks.

Those three compete for the same technicians every morning, and by ten the plan has already changed. The board is the part of an elevator company that everything else waits on.

In ESS the dispatch board shows real jobs on real units, with technician locations from GPS underneath it. When a callback lands, the dispatcher can see who is closest, what else that person owes the day, and which route work can move without putting a test date at risk. Nothing has to be rebuilt on a magnet board afterwards, because the board is the record.

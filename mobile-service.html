In the technician's hand

Four things the app settles before anyone calls the office.

Mobile Service is built around the way an elevator route actually runs: unfamiliar units, code-driven tests, callbacks that land mid-morning, and buildings that swallow the signal.

Unit history

The unit's record, in hand

Every prior visit on that car, the MCP, and the results of the last category test open on the phone. The tech arrives knowing what was replaced, what was flagged and what is due next.

Routing

An optimized route, not a list

The day arrives in the order that makes sense for where the units are and where the crew is. When a callback comes in, dispatch drops it into the nearest gap and the rest of the route reflows.

Time

Electronic time sheets

Hours are recorded against the unit and the work order while the job is happening, instead of being rebuilt from memory on Friday afternoon. This is the piece that cut payroll prep in half at Elevator One.

Offline

Offline in the hoistway

Machine rooms, hoistways, pits and remote sites drop the signal. Notes, parts, photos and signatures are captured anyway, held on the device, and sent the moment the phone reconnects.

One visit, end to end

From the first stop to the invoice, without a second keyboard.

A scheduled MCP visit at a mid-rise, two cars in the same machine room. Here is what the tech touches, and what happens when the signal disappears halfway through it.

01
The day opens on the phone in route order, with both units in that building on the same stop.
02
Before he walks in, the tech reads the MCP, the last category 1 result and the last three callbacks on car 2.
03
Status moves to en route and then on site from the phone, so nobody in the office has to ring round to find him.
04
Work on the car is recorded as it happens, and parts come off truck stock as they go on.
05
Down in the machine room the signal dies. The deficiency he finds on car 2, the photos, the parts, the time and the customer signature are captured offline.
06
Back above ground the phone syncs on its own. Hours to the time sheet, parts to inventory, the deficiency to the office as quotable repair work, invoice out the same day.

The tech never sees the sync. There is no export, no upload step and no second app to reopen at the end of the day, which is why the offline mode holds up on a route that spends its afternoon in basements.

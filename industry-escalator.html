Industry · Escalator

Escalators and moving walks run on the same unit-level service model.

A serial-numbered unit. An inspection cadence set by code. A multi-year contract behind it, and a callback history that has to stay attached to the machine. ESS was built for that pattern in the hoistway, and it holds at the truss.

Same model

What carries over from elevator, unchanged.

Most contractors who service escalators already service cars. None of this has to become a second system: same records, same calendar, same contracts, same field app in the technician’s hand.

01

Unit-level equipment records

Every escalator and moving walk is its own record. Serial number, manufacturer, controller, drive, rise, location in the building and install date, with the full work history hanging off it. When a technician opens a unit in Mobile Service, the last visit is already there.

  • Drive, controller and step type held on the unit
  • History attached to the machine, not to the address
  • Readable at the truss with no signal
02

Code-driven inspection cadence

Inspections are scheduled off the code calendar, not off someone’s memory. Due dates roll forward as work is closed, the office sees what falls due in the next thirty days, and the signed paperwork stays attached to the unit that was tested.

  • Due dates generated from the agreement
  • Thirty-day view of what is coming up
  • Signed reports filed against the unit
03

Multi-year service contracts

Coverage terms, billing schedule, escalation dates and renewal dates live on the contract. You can read profitability on an escalator contract the same way you read it on an MCP book, before the renewal conversation instead of after it.

  • Escalations and renewal dates on the agreement
  • Units added and removed mid-term
  • Contract margin, not just contract revenue
04

Callbacks on a named unit

A callback is logged against unit 4 at the north entrance, not against the mall. Repeat-callback history is visible before the truck rolls, and labor and parts cost back to that unit and that contract.

  • Ticket opened on the machine, with its history
  • Repeat callbacks visible per unit and per site
  • Labor and parts costed to the contract
What is different

Steps, combs, handrails and the hours you are allowed to work.

The service model carries over. The checklist and the calendar do not. ESS lets you set the inspection forms, the parts and the scheduling rules that escalator work actually needs.

Steps and palletsStep and pallet inspections as their own checklist: step chain, rollers, demarcation, gaps, damaged or missing steps. Findings come back from the field as part of the work order, with photos, not as a note someone retypes on Monday.
Combs and handrailsComb plate teeth, comb impact devices, handrail speed and entry guards get checked on their own cadence. Recurring findings on one unit are visible in its history, which is what turns a repeat repair into a modernization quote.
Heavy-traffic sitesRetail centers, airports and transit stations run units hard and watch them closely. Property managers see the equipment list, upcoming inspections and open work for their own sites in the Customer Portal, without calling your office.
Shutdown windowsAn escalator out of service blocks a floor. Work gets scheduled around trading hours, night windows and mall management approvals, and the dispatch board shows the crew the window they have, not just the address.
A night window

A scheduled inspection at a retail center, booking to invoice.

Escalator work is rarely convenient. The unit has to come out of service, the center will give you a window, and everything after that depends on whether the crew arrives with the right record in front of them.

01The contract sets the date. The inspection is generated from the agreement covering that center, against the four units it lists, months before anyone has to remember it.
02Dispatch books it into the window the center allows, not into an empty slot on a Tuesday. The technician sees the access time and the site contact on the job, alongside the address.
03The crew works the unit. Mobile Service opens unit 4 with its last inspection, its open findings and the step and comb checklist, and it keeps working when the machine space has no signal.
04A finding becomes the next job. A worn comb plate or a step chain out of tolerance is captured with photos and turns into a quote on the same unit, instead of a line in a report nobody reads until the renewal.
05The office bills it that day. Time, parts and the signed report arrive attached to the work order, so the invoice goes out without waiting for paperwork to come in off a truck.
06The center reads it themselves. The report is in the Document Library and the next visit is on the schedule in the Customer Portal, which is one fewer call into your office on Monday.

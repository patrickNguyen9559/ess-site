Customer Portal

The portal more RFPs are asking for.

Property managers and building owners want to see their own equipment, their own inspection dates and their own invoices without calling your office first. The Customer Portal gives them that view, drawn from the records your office and field teams are already keeping.

Why it is on the bid sheet

Bid packages now ask what your customers can see.

Portfolio owners are used to self-service everywhere else in their week. When two contractors quote the same building, the one whose customers can answer their own questions is the easier yes.

Every area of the portal reads from Mobile Office Manager, so there is nothing to export, upload or keep in step by hand.

Four areas

What your customer sees when they log in.

The portal is organized the way a building owner thinks about the relationship: my equipment, my schedule, my bill, my paperwork.

Customer Manager

Equipment, MCPs and history in one view

Every unit ESS holds for that customer, listed by building, address and controller, with the maintenance control program that covers it. Under each unit sits the work that has happened on it: routine visits, callbacks, repairs and modernization, with the technician notes your office already sees.

Schedule Manager

Visible upcoming inspections and visits

Scheduled maintenance, booked repair work and the next category test appear with dates attached, per building and per unit. When dispatch moves a visit the customer sees the new date, so the calendar in their head matches the one on your dispatch board.

Billing Manager

Invoices, statements and payment online

Open invoices, paid history and account statements, each line traceable back to the work order that produced it. Customers can review a charge against the visit behind it and pay online, which shortens the gap between finishing the job and getting paid for it.

Document Library

Certificates, test reports and sign-offs

Inspection certificates, category test reports, signed work orders and contract paperwork, filed against the building they belong to. The owner downloads what they need at the hour they need it, including the night before an inspection walk or an audit.

What it takes off the office

The calls that stop coming in.

Nobody budgets for interruptions, and they still cost a coordinator most of an afternoon every week. These are the ones the portal answers on its own.

Status calls"Did anyone come out to 1245 Park Ave last week?" The customer opens the unit and reads the visit themselves: who attended, what they found and what is still open.
Emailed certificatesNo more searching the shared drive for a signed test report and attaching it to a reply. The certificate is filed in the Document Library against the building, the moment it is signed off.
"When is my cat test?"The date shows on the customer schedule as soon as dispatch books it, and the report appears in their library when the inspection closes.
Invoice copiesStatements, open invoices and paid history are already in front of them, with the work order behind each line. Payment happens in the portal instead of in a follow-up call.
Manager handoversA building changes property managers and the new one logs in to the same record. Your coordinator does not have to rebuild two years of service history in an email thread.
We Solve, You Succeed!

The portal is a view of your database, not a copy of it.

Mobile Office Manager, Mobile Service and the Customer Portal share one database, so work captured at the unit shows up everywhere it matters, including on your customer’s screen.

01A technician closes a category 1 test in Mobile Service and collects the sign-off at the unit, offline if the hoistway has no signal.
02The office sees the completed visit, the certificate and the billable time in Mobile Office Manager, and invoices it the same day.
03The property manager opens the portal and the visit, the report and the invoice are already there. Nobody published them.

Because there is no export step, there is no second version of the truth to keep in step. The portal cannot go stale while a coordinator is on vacation, and a customer can never be looking at a document your office has since replaced.
